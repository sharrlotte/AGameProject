# Block Art Spec

## Purpose

All block sprites SHALL be generated by one deterministic system (`tools/gen_block_art.py`) so that individually drawn pieces read as parts of a single modular spacecraft manufacturing system when assembled into a ship. This document defines the art direction, lighting, continuity rules, construction standard, category language and palette.

## Scope

This spec governs only the sprite assets under `core/assets/texture/block/`. Asset filenames, pixel dimensions, alpha conventions and references from `config/base/block.ron` SHALL NOT change. Ship layouts, block dimensions, grid coordinates, gameplay logic and rendering code are out of scope.

## Art direction

The visual target is a top-down / slightly angled sci-fi industrial spacecraft: futuristic military hardware, solid metallic surfaces, clean geometric panel construction, subtle mechanical detail, restrained cyan and blue emissive elements, muted gunmetal primary material, and gold or bronze accents on selected components.

### Asset Visual Standard

All assets SHALL NOT look cartoon-like and MUST adhere to the following rules:

- **Aesthetic**: Grounded sci-fi industrial hardware; never cartoon-like or stylized with comic outlines.
- **Palette**: Limited, intentional colors drawn strictly from the defined palette.
- **Outlines**: Consistent thickness across all assets; no heavy black borders on structural blocks so tiles connect seamlessly.
- **Lighting**: One consistent light direction (upper-left) across every asset without exception.
- **Gradients**: Avoid unnecessary gradients; use crisp geometric transitions, clean matte fills, and defined panel lines.
- **Highlights**: Small and purposeful; restrained 1px-2px sheen on illuminated edges and bolt tops.
- **Details**: Functional, not random; all rivets, bolts, vents, and seams indicate real mechanical assembly.
- **Texture**: Consistent pixel density across the entire game; draw directly at native resolution with no mixed resolutions.
- **Silhouette**: Distinct and readable at native resolution against both empty space and adjacent ship blocks.

## Lighting and shading

One global light source SHALL be used for every sprite: upper-left. Consequences:

- Upper-left edges receive a subtle highlight in `EDGE_LIGHT`.
- Lower-right edges receive darker shading in `SHADOW`.
- Bevel depth and shadow intensity SHALL be identical across all blocks.
- Material roughness SHALL be identical across the same category.
- No sprite SHALL introduce an independent lighting direction.

Triangle art is authored in its base orientation; placement rotation rotates the finished sprite together with its lighting. This is accepted because the light is a stylistic bevel rather than a world-space shadow.

## Block-to-block continuity

Continuity is the highest priority. Sprites SHALL tile into continuous armor:

- No thick dark outline around each individual block.
- No unnecessary dark border between adjacent structural blocks.
- Panel seams SHALL use the same inset, color and width on every block.
- Bevels SHALL stop at the panel boundary rather than carving a gap between blocks.
- Edge highlights and shadows SHALL NOT create artificial gaps when two blocks touch.
- Individual blocks remain distinguishable through seam placement, rivets and accent details.

## Construction standard

- Every sprite SHALL be drawn directly at its final pixel dimensions. No half-resolution draw or `NEAREST` upscale SHALL be used, because it degrades triangle alpha edges.
- Every visible detail SHALL be at least 2 pixels wide. Detail scale, outer margin, corner chamfer, bevel width and panel-line width SHALL be expressed in final pixels and reused across assets.
- Structural blocks use a 1-pixel chamfer highlight and a single restrained shadow edge.
- Armor blocks use a heavier plate with reinforced corners and bronze rivets.
- Power blocks use mechanical housings around cyan or blue emissive windows.
- Weapon barrels use metallic housings with clear barrel geometry and transparent backgrounds.
- Utility blocks use functional industrial detail with lower visual emphasis.

## Category language

- **Structure** — clean gunmetal plate, subtle seams, restrained edges, gold or bronze chamfer accents on triangle pieces.
- **Armor** — heavier plating, X-braces or panel splits, reinforced corners, bronze fasteners.
- **Power** — cyan or blue emissive core, subtle glow ring, mechanical frame.
- **Weapons** — defined barrels or housings, consistent gunmetal material, cyan for energy weapons, amber or red for thermal and missile systems.
- **Utility** — compact industrial housings, vents and functional indicators, less visual emphasis.

## Generation

`python tools/gen_block_art.py` regenerates every sprite in `core/assets/texture/block/`. The generator SHALL remain the single source of truth: no asset SHALL be hand-edited in a way that the generator cannot reproduce. Triangle alpha masks SHALL use the same half-plane inequalities as `triangle_cells` in `content/block/component.rs`.

## Validation

Sprites SHALL be validated as assembled ships, not only in isolation. Use the shipped `Interceptor` and `Raider` designs in the ship editor, with the exact same layout and camera before and after an art change. Verify:

- adjacent metallic surfaces blend without dark seams or gaps;
- lighting direction is identical on every block;
- detail scale is consistent across block categories;
- turret barrels retain transparent backgrounds and read as weapon hardware;
- triangle pieces continue to align with their hitboxes;
- the complete ship reads as one cohesive spacecraft.
